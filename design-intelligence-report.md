## 1. DESIGN PHILOSOPHY (The Mindset)

These are not rules. They are attitudes every poster should embody.

**1. Design is emotional evidence, not decoration.**
The poster's job is not to announce an event. It is to make someone feel the event before it happens — the specific anticipation of *this* dinner vs. any dinner. Borrow from Chris Do's principle: a brand is a gut feeling. Every Jalsa card must trigger a specific gut feeling, not a general "this looks nice."

**2. Human imperfection is the flex.**
In 2026, flawless = AI = forgettable. The dominant counter-signal in design right now is what they call "tactile rebellion": deliberate roughness, grain, texture, printing errors reframed as character. Jalsa cards should look like they were *made*, not generated.

**3. India is not a motif. It is a material.**
The mistake most Indian apps make: they either go full Western minimalism (safe, bland) or slap peacocks and mandalas on everything (tourist kitch). The move — demonstrated by Studio Bigfat's tri-script Subko branding and Ek Type's multi-script work — is to use Indian design *language* (dense layering, bold color confidence, script mixing) without Indian design *clichés* (lotus, rangoli pattern fills, Bollywood gold).

**4. Context precision over generic cool.**
A coffee meetup at Dyu Art Café in Koramangala is not the same vibe as a house party in HSR Layout. Jalsa's AI system must read the *specific* event type and produce a card that could only be for that event. Generic "event poster" energy is the enemy.

**5. The three-tier system is about energy, not status.**
Whisper is not lesser than Unhinged. Each tier is a complete, self-consistent design language. Do not let tier 1 look like tier 3 with the volume turned down — they should feel like three different studios made them.

**6. Typography does the heavy lifting.**
Across every credible source reviewed — Fontspring trends, It's Nice That, Typewolf — the conclusion is identical: Gen Z design is led by type. Color and texture support the type. The type IS the poster.

**7. Anti-originality is the originality trap.**
The biggest risk for an AI poster system: defaulting to what "looks like a Gen Z poster." That produces the median, not the distinctive. The system should ask: what would feel *specific* here, not what would feel *trendy*.

## 2. THE AESTHETIC SPECTRUM (3 Tiers)

### TIER 1 — WHISPER (Clean & Quiet)
*For: coffee meetups, dinner plans, study sessions, road trips, casual hangouts*

**Typography approach:**
- Primary: **Editorial New** (Pangram Pangram) or **Migra** — editorial weight without Didot cliché. Large, tracked-out caps. Set headline at 80–120pt, tracking +80 to +120.
- Secondary: **DM Sans** (Google Fonts, free) — clean, slightly warm, not corporate. 14–16pt, Regular only — never Bold at this tier.
- Relationship: Headline takes 60% of vertical space. Details small, placed in one corner, not centered.

**Color logic:**
- Two-color maximum. One warm neutral (off-white, warm cream, desaturated sand) + one muted chromatic accent (dusty sage, slate blue, faded terracotta). Both colors should look slightly washed.
- Never pure white (#FFFFFF) — shift it 5% warm. Never pure black — use very dark brown-black (#1a1208).
- Background is always the dominant surface.

**Layout rules:**
- Single column. No grid complexity.
- Maximum 4 typographic elements.
- At least 40% of the card surface is blank.
- Thin horizontal rule (0.5pt) to separate event name from details. That's your only graphic element.
- Portrait 4:5 ratio.

**Texture/material:**
- Subtle paper grain overlay at 8–12% opacity. Cold-press watercolor paper scan, not noise filter.
- Optionally: micro letterpress impression on headline — 3% drop shadow inward, no outer glow.
- No illustration. No photography. Type + surface only.

**What to NEVER do at Tier 1:**
- No gradients of any kind
- No more than 2 fonts
- No centered text
- No drop shadows
- No icons or emojis embedded
- No bright accent colors (no orange, coral, hot pink)
- No all-lowercase headline

**AI Prompt Template (Tier 1):**
```
Minimal typographic event invite card, editorial print aesthetic, [BACKGROUND_COLOR: warm linen #F5EFE0] paper texture background with cold-press watercolor grain overlay at 10% opacity, [ACCENT_COLOR: muted sage green #8FA886] single accent element, large [EVENT_NAME] in Editorial New Italic or Playfair Display Italic 90pt tracked display headline left-aligned top-third, small DM Sans Regular 12pt detail text bottom-left corner listing [DATE] [TIME] [LOCATION], abundant negative space occupying 40% of surface, single thin 0.5pt horizontal rule separating headline from details, no illustration no photography no icons no gradient, letterpress impression feel, Kinfolk magazine / Cereal magazine editorial aesthetic, portrait 4:5 ratio, 2-color palette only, quiet confident minimal design --ar 4:5 --style raw --s 50 --v 7
```

### TIER 2 — FLEX (Confident & Cool)
*For: birthday dinners, rooftop parties, weekend trips, college socials, sports watch parties*

**Typography approach:**
- Primary: **Syne** (Google Fonts, free) or **Space Grotesk** (Google Fonts, free). Syne especially has variable-weight playfulness: use ExtraBold for event name, slight rotation (2–4 degrees max).
- Secondary: **Fragment Mono** (Google Fonts, free) — monospaced, slightly retro-tech. Set at 11–13pt, all caps, tight tracking.
- Relationship: Event name can break across two lines with visual tension — different weight on each line (ExtraBold + Light) using Syne variable font.

**Color logic:**
- Three colors maximum. Logic: one dark base (near-black, deep navy, or deep forest green), one bold chromatic mid-tone (mustard, rust, olive), one bright pop accent used only on 1–2 words.
- Dark base should occupy 50–60% of the surface.
- Bengaluru nightlife palette: exposed brick + warm tungsten + neon sign = deep brown-black + amber-warm + electric pop.
- Background luminosity: 8–25% (dark is correct at this tier).

**Layout rules:**
- Diagonal or asymmetric text placement — one deliberate axis.
- One graphical element permitted: simple geometric shape OR single texture block.
- Text can overlap graphic element deliberately.
- Allow one element to bleed off the edge.
- 3–6 typographic elements total.

**Texture/material:**
- Riso-style duotone: simulate risograph printing with slight misregistration (1–3px offset).
- Grain at 18–25% opacity — more visible than Tier 1.
- Optionally: halftone dot pattern in corner at 15% opacity.

**What to NEVER do at Tier 2:**
- No stock illustration (Canva-style clipart is death)
- No gradient fills on text
- No more than 3 colors
- No symmetrical layouts
- No centered text blocks
- No Times New Roman, Georgia, or Merriweather

**AI Prompt Template (Tier 2):**
```

```

### TIER 3 — UNHINGED (Full Chaos Energy)
*For: house parties, raves, concert nights, festival pre-parties, NYE events*

**Typography approach:**
- Primary: **Bebas Neue** (Google Fonts, free) stacked at 200–300pt and overlapping itself, OR **Migra Extrabold Italic** at extreme scale. The type is architecture.
- Secondary: **Space Mono** (Google Fonts, free) at tiny scale (8–9pt) in dense blocks, functioning as texture, not information.
- Allow type to rotate, bleed, layer over itself. Multiple type sizes on same line (150pt next to 12pt).
- Maximum 3 different typefaces, each serving a different content role (headline / info / texture).

**Color logic:**
- Maximalist color logic with structure: one extreme neon (acid green #B5FF3A, electric magenta #FF00FF, or fluorescent orange #FF4D00) + one pure dark (near-black or deep midnight purple) + one unexpected tertiary (baby blue, chrome silver, or cream).
- Alternatively: go full monochrome — all-black with white type and one acid-yellow single word.
- Neon colors should glow: subtle outer glow (4px, 30% opacity) same hue. NOT white glow.
- Indian color boldness applies: push saturation to 95–100% on accent colors.

**Layout rules:**
- Organized chaos: everything looks wrong, but structured wrong *deliberately*.
- Overlapping text blocks required.
- At least one element extends beyond card boundary.
- Multiple orientations of text on same card.
- Allow collage-style layering: photo fragments, texture scans, hand-drawn marks.
- Zine aesthetic: imagine this was photocopied 3 times and gained something each pass.

**Texture/material:**
- Heavy grain (40–60% opacity noise layer) is mandatory.
- Xerox/photocopy degradation: halation around bright type on dark ground.
- Riso-style misregistration pushed further: 4–8px offset.
- Optionally: burn/dodge texture overlay to simulate screen-printed on recycled card stock.

**What to NEVER do at Tier 3:**
- No clean gradients
- No Poppins
- No geometric Flaticon-style icons
- Do not mistake "more colors" for chaos
- No centered, balanced compositions
- Never let the event name become unreadable

**AI Prompt Template (Tier 3):**
```
Brutalist zine rave poster event flyer, [BASE_COLOR: maximum black #080808] background, heavy photocopy grain noise texture 50% opacity overlay, [EVENT_NAME] in Bebas Neue 280pt overlapping and bleeding off right and bottom edges, multiple overlapping text blocks, secondary Space Mono 7pt dense text block used as texture element listing [EVENT_DETAILS] in tight block, [NEON_COLOR: acid green #B5FF3A] as primary type color with 4px outer glow halo effect same hue, [ACCENT_COLOR: electric magenta #FF2D78] applied to single unexpected word, Syne ExtraBold 40pt rotated 90-degrees left side as structural element, 2px risograph misregistration on all color layers, xerox/photocopy degradation halation around type, organized chaos aesthetic, text at multiple scales (280pt next to 8pt), Indian street poster energy meets NY underground rave flyer meets Tokyo zine culture, raw human-made not AI-polished feel, portrait 4:5 ratio --ar 4:5 --style raw --s 250 --chaos 35 --v 7
```

## 4. COLOR PALETTE FRAMEWORK

### Tier 1 Logic — Desaturate Everything 30%
Your instinct says terracotta — use dusty rose. Forest green — use sage. Colors should look slightly bleached, as if pinned to a sunny café wall for two weeks. Maximum saturation across palette: 55%. Background luminosity: 88–95%.

### Tier 2 Logic — One Color Should Be "Wrong"
Build a palette where two colors are harmonious and one creates deliberate friction. The "wrong" color earns its place by appearing only once, only on the most important word. Background luminosity: 8–25% (dark is correct at this tier).

### Tier 3 Logic — Maximum Saturation, Minimum Colors
2–3 colors maximum, each at 90–100% saturation. The chaos comes from layout and texture, not from a rainbow. This is how NH7 Weekender, underground rave posters, and Superflux's speculative design all work.

### Indian Color Sensibility Notes

- **Indian design has a cultural tolerance for maximum saturation** that Western European design does not. Using #FF2D78 at full saturation on a Tier 3 card will look "too much" to a Scandinavian designer and exactly right to someone from Bengaluru.
- **Warm neutrals outperform cool neutrals.** In Indian interiors and fashion, the default "neutral" is warm — cream, sand, warm grey — not the cool grey that dominates Swiss design tradition. Apply this to Tier 1.
- **Gold (#FFD700, #C9A84C) is a prestige signal** in Indian visual culture. Use at Tier 2 for high-status events (engagement dinners, milestone birthdays). Never at Tier 1 (too heavy), once max at Tier 3 (the unexpected accent).
- **Bright pink (#FF2D78, #FF0099) reads as celebratory, not feminine,** in Indian visual culture. It is festive. Use it at Tier 3 wherever there is celebration.
- **Avoid brown-dominant palettes alone** — without a clear chromatic accent, brown reads as "unprofessional" to Indian Gen Z (association with cheap print shops).

## 5. BENGALURU SCENE NOTES

### What the Bengaluru Creative Crowd Responds To

The 22–28 cohort centered in Koramangala, Indiranagar, and HSR Layout is simultaneously the most globally connected and locally rooted Indian design audience. They follow Are.na and Cosmos, know Studio Bigfat and November's work, have strong opinions about typefaces, and immediately clock when something looks like it came from a Freepik template.

Key visual signatures that resonate:
- **Type-led design** — the typography *is* the design, not a caption on an image
- **Earthy + electric combinations** — the Bengaluru climate creates instinct for warm, dusty colors broken by tech-bright accents (reflects the city: startup + garden city)
- **Industrial-warm aesthetic** — exposed brick, raw concrete, warm tungsten — the visual language of The Humming Tree
- **Multilingual design moments** — a word in Kannada script within an otherwise English design signals cultural fluency, not tokenism

**Authentic in Bengaluru context:**
- Kannada script used deliberately (as content, not decoration)

- New York loft minimalism applied to South Indian event culture
- Cherry blossom or neon Tokyo references for a Koramangala house party
- Generic "Indian" design: lotus patterns, Rajasthani miniature pastiche, saffron-green-white color blocking
- Y2K chrome aesthetics without any cultural localization

## 6. DESIGN CRIME LIST (What NEVER to Do)

These are immediate signals of creative unseriousness to a Gen Z Indian design audience.

1. **Poppins as the headline font.** Font of every Indian EdTech and LinkedIn thought leadership carousel from 2019–2023. Will make your card look like a BYJU'S push notification.

2. **Stock vector illustrations from Freepik or Flaticon.** Immediately identifiable. The "people shaking hands" illustration, "calendar with a star" icon — all are death.

3. **Gradient text fills.** The multicolor rainbow gradient on the headline. Instagram DM aesthetic, 2017. Completely gone.

4. **Drop shadows on everything.** White drop shadow on colored text, black drop shadow on white text. Signals the designer discovered layer effects and applied them without restraint.

5. **Indian flag colors (saffron + white + green) for any non-patriotic event.** It reads as template downloaded for Independence Day.

6. **Lotuses, paisleys, and mandalas as decorative fill.** What happens when non-Indian designers try to make "Indian-feeling" design. Indian Gen Z actively rejects this.

7. **Center-aligned everything.** Centered headline, subhead, date, time — entire card on a vertical axis. Formal invitation design from 2005.

8. **Beveled or embossed text.** The PowerPoint 3D effect. No context where this is acceptable.

9. **Using Comic Sans "ironically."** The irony has been fully used up. Now just reads as ignorant.

10. **The "confetti on white background" composition.** Floating stars, sparkles, small shapes randomly distributed over a white card. This is Canva's "celebration" template. Gen Z identifies it in 0.3 seconds.

11. **Over-kerned lowercase in Futura or Gotham.** The "l u x u r y" spacing. Signals aspirational-middle not actually-cool.

12. **Three or more gradients in the same palette.** Design equivalent of wearing every item in your wardrobe simultaneously.

13. **Using Impact or Arial Black as the "edgy" choice.** Microsoft Word defaults. Use Bebas Neue or Syne ExtraBold instead.

14. **Photography with heavy Instagram filter (Valencia, Sierra, Nashville).** Over-saturated, purple-shadow filter aesthetic is deeply 2014. Either go full editorial (clean, minimal) or heavy grain-textured (deliberate degradation) — never the saturated-filter middle ground.

15. **The multicolor emoji border.** Placing 🎉🎊🥳 as design elements on card boundary. This is WhatsApp forwards, not design.

16. **White text, thin weight, on a photographic background without treatment.** Unreadable. Minimum: darken photo to 60% + add a text treatment.

17. **Mixing sans-serif and serif without a clear logic.** The test: if you can't explain why these two fonts are together, they are wrong together.

18. **"Hearty Congratulations" in Brush Script.** Religious/festive event design cliché. Even at a shaadi or puja event, there are better approaches.
